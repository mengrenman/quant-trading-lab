How to read this page

  • Views: Trade has the chart, order entry, the order book and the tape; Analyze measures this run (the order-book heatmap, stylized facts, order flow imbalance); Learn has the lessons and this guide; Pairs is a market of its own, two order books whose gap mean-reverts, with pairs traders in them; Stat-arb is another, many books with several common factors, with traders of many relations. The simulation controls and the top of the book stay in view on the first three, and the panels take as many columns as the window has room for. Text, in the header, sets the size of everything on the page.
  • Candles are built from executed trades on the simulation clock, which advances with each model's event times and by 0.5 per manual action. Intervals without trades are drawn flat at the last price, with zero volume.
  • Volume is split by the side that initiated each trade: buys in green, sells in red (blue and orange with color-blind-safe colors).
  • Depth of market shows 10 price levels on each side of the mid. Click a level to copy its price into the order form; with 1-click on, a click places a limit order there instead (a buy on a bid level, a sell on an ask level, for the size in the limit form), and the book holds still while you aim. Your resting orders are marked "you"; drag that badge to another level to move them there (they go to the back of its queue). Ticks, in its header, shows instead every price near the mid at fixed rows, bids on the left and asks on the right: rows do not shift as levels empty and fill, and one-click trading and dragging work at any price, empty ones included (Center brings the mid back to the middle). Under it, market depth draws the lots bid and offered, added up from the mid outward over a wider range: a large resting order is a cliff in its side's curve, and the gap between the curves at the bottom is the spread.
  • Your orders stay until they fill or you cancel them: the models cancel only their own. On the chart, a dashed line marks each price where you have orders resting, and an arrow each bar in which you traded, as maker or taker (below the bar for buying, above it for selling), with the lots and average price. My Active Orders shows each order's place in its queue: the lots ahead of it, or with pro-rata matching its share of the level.
  • Your P&L, in ticks times lots (one lot gaining one tick is 1): the strip at the top and the card under My Active Orders show your position, the profit or loss realized by selling out of it at more or less than its average price, the unrealized part marked to the mid, and your edge against the mid as a resting and as an aggressive trader. The line under the volume chart shows it over time, marked to each bar's close.
  • Strategies (at the bottom of Trade): up to {{ max_strategies }} quantitative strategies of yours at once, each trading with an account of its own while the simulation runs. Making markets: market making (quotes around the mid), Avellaneda-Stoikov (around a reservation price, by volatility and risk aversion) and penny jumping (a tick ahead of the best quotes). Trading on a signal: momentum, mean reversion, breakout, queue imbalance and order flow imbalance, each by crossing the spread or by resting at the best quote. And two benchmarks: a random trader, which knows nothing, and a quote joiner, the null for making markets. Their orders are marked in the order book with their tags. The leaderboard ranks them, and you, by P&L, with what each holds, the lots it traded, its edge against the mid at each fill, the markout (the same 100 events later) and the difference, adverse selection. It holds still while the pointer is on a row's buttons. The line above it says whether the order flow pays resting orders: in these models it does, so a resting strategy earns without skill. Vs null holds each strategy's fills against one with no skill on the same side at the same time: the model's market orders for its taking fills, and the quote joiner for its resting ones (Add the null). Fit it over a training period tries the combinations of the values you give for its settings on the same period, a fresh run of the model from now or a recording (when there are more than {{ max_fit_candidates }}, a random sample of them), best first by P&L or by P&L per unit of risk; start the one you like, and its row keeps what it made in training to compare with what it makes afterwards. Strategies trade in a facts collection too, and Reset starts each one over.
  • Pairs traders (on Pairs): up to {{ pairs_max_traders }} at once, of three kinds: a z-score at a fixed hedge, a Kalman filter that learns its hedge, and a copula on the books' returns. Fit it over training runs tries your settings and the combinations of the values you give (when there are more than {{ max_fit_candidates - 1 }}, or too many for their length, a random sample) in new markets from the books' settings, on seeds the market on the chart can never have, best first by P&L or by P&L per unit of risk. Each is shown against your settings, with a standard error, and the panel says whether the best is clearly better, by two of them; often it is not, and then nothing says to change. Start the one you like, and its row keeps what it made in training a unit of time, to compare with what it makes afterwards.
  • Stat-arb: up to 12 books, whose fair values share several common factors: sectors, where the pairs within a sector are cointegrated, or loadings in no order, where no pair is. Any portfolio that leaves the factors out mean-reverts, and the status line says how many independent relations that makes. Up to {{ pairs_max_traders }} traders at once: many pairs, chosen by how close they stayed or by Engle-Granger; baskets from the Johansen test; stat-arb on PCA residuals (Avellaneda and Lee 2010); many pairs through copulas; and four that score every book, buy the best and sell the worst, neutral to the factors: reversal, momentum, lead-lag and order flow. Each chooses from the past mids before it trades, and a new one can learn from what has gone by. The chart shows the signal of every relation of the trader you pick, what it holds, and how much of the factors that takes on. What the tests see runs the Johansen test and Engle-Granger on the last 10,000 units and sets what they find beside what is there. Fit it over training runs works as on Pairs, in new markets of the same books: the more books, the fewer settings it tries. Only one fit runs at a time, in either view. Loadings can drift, so that no fixed relation stays one. Notebook 12 says what to expect: a pair need not be exactly cointegrated to pay, small orders in many relations cost far less and swing far less than one large one, and when the loadings drift everything fades.
  • Models: the bar under the speed control describes the current one. With informed traders (ZI with informed traders) that bar also says what share of the model's market-order lots they sent and what makers lose to them, which only a simulation can know; Reveal, on the chart's toolbar, draws the hidden value they trade on (a recording does not hold it).
  • Order-book heatmap (Analyze) shows the visible depth near the mid over the last 600 units of simulation time, with the trades and the mid-price: large resting orders are bright bands, and Reveal hidden outlines iceberg reserves, which the market cannot see. Hover over it for the numbers. It starts over after a reset or a collection.
  • Recordings: Record (next to Reset Book) keeps the book as it stands and everything that happens next, yours and your strategies' orders included, until you stop it ({{ "{:,}".format(max_recording_events) }} events at most). Play it back from Analyze: the run comes back exactly, at any speed, and the timeline moves through it; Rewind goes back to its start. Trading or starting strategies during a playback tries them on the same market, which then goes its own way; Exit playback carries on live from there, with the model selected. Download keeps a recording as a .npz file (a notebook can read it with numpy), to upload and play back later.
  • Stylized facts (Analyze) measures the current model as notebook 05 does. Collect runs 400,000 events in a few seconds; changing the model, its settings, the engine or the book starts a new sample.
